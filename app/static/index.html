Roast classification and defect detection for coffee beans

Upload a photo of a coffee bean. BeanSight first classifies the roast level, then runs the defect model that matches the result: a 17-class classifier for green (unroasted) beans, or a YOLOv8 detector that finds broken, quaker and scorched beans in roasted coffee.

How it works

Three ONNX models, run on CPU, chained by the result of the first one.

1

Roast level

An EfficientNet-B0 classifier labels the bean as Green, Light, Medium or Dark. The result is shown and also decides which defect model runs next.

Model
EfficientNet-B0
Classes
4
Input
224 × 224
Green Roasted
2A

Green bean defect classifier

For unroasted beans, a second EfficientNet-B0 classifies the bean into one of 17 defect classes such as Full Black, Fungus Damage, Insect Damage, Parchment or Withered. Works on a single bean per photo.

Model
EfficientNet-B0
Classes
17
Scope
Single bean
2B

Roasted bean defect detector

For roasted beans, a YOLOv8n detector locates every bean and tags each one as Normal, Broken, Quaker or Scorched, so it handles a single bean or a handful at once.

Model
YOLOv8n
Classes
4
Scope
One or many beans

Models were trained on public coffee bean datasets and exported to ONNX; accuracy is best on photos that resemble the training data (a single bean on a plain background for classification). Predictions are informational and not a substitute for a certified grader.
